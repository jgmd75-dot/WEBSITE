Dynamics

Tandem

Two RMS soft-knee compressors in series on one panel.

Overview

Tandem puts two classic RMS / VCA compressors one after the other. Each stage does a little of the work, which is the old studio trick for heavy compression that still sounds open: the first stage levels the performance gently, the second catches what gets through.

Both stages measure loudness (RMS) rather than peaks, so they react the way your ears do. There are no attack and release knobs - the timing follows the music, as on the original hardware: the further the signal goes over the threshold, the faster the stage reacts, and it recovers at a steady pace.

Reach for it for

  • Vocals that need to sit still in a mix
  • Drum and mix buses
  • Bass guitar
  • Ducking a music bed under a voice (EXT KEY)
  • Creative pumping and reverse-dynamics effects (negative ratios)

Controls

Stage 1 and Stage 2

The two stages have the same main controls. The signal goes through Stage 1, then Stage 2.

IN
Switches the stage in or out. With a stage out, the audio passes straight through it.
THRESHOLD (red cap)
The level where the stage starts working, from -60 to 0 dB. Turn it down (left) for more compression. Because the detector measures loudness, the threshold relates to the average level rather than the peaks.
RATIO (yellow cap)
How strongly the stage compresses once the level is over the threshold. 1:1 does nothing; 2:1 and 4:1 are moderate; INF:1 (marked in red) is limiting - nothing gets louder. Past INF the ratio becomes negative (-2:1, -1:1): the louder the input, the quieter the output. That reverses the dynamics, for pumping and swelling effects.
OUTPUT (blue cap)
The stage's make-up gain, from -20 to +20 dB. Use it to bring the level back up after compression.
BELOW / KNEE / ABOVE
Three lights showing where the level sits: green below the threshold, amber in the soft knee, red above it.
GR meter
The row of 12 lights under the knobs shows the gain reduction of that stage, from 1 to 30 dB.
HARD
Off (default): a soft knee - the ratio eases in gradually over 10 dB around the threshold, which is smoother and more transparent. On: the full ratio starts right at the threshold, for a firmer, more obvious effect.

Stage 1 only

DECOUPLE
Off: both sides of the stereo signal get exactly the same gain, so the stereo image stays still. On: each side mostly follows its own level (80% its own, 20% shared), like unlinking a bus compressor. The sound becomes wider and livelier, but the image can move a little.
EXT KEY
Stage 1 listens to the key input (plug-in pins 3 and 4) instead of the audio it is compressing. Use it for ducking: route a voice or kick to pins 3/4 and the music dips whenever it plays. Stage 2 always listens to Stage 1's output.
KEY IN
Lights when a signal arrives on the key input - a quick check that your routing works.

Stage 2 only

GRIT
Drives Stage 2's VCA into gentle 2nd and 3rd harmonic distortion. The harder Stage 2 works, the grittier it gets, so it adds attitude on the loud parts and stays clean on the quiet ones.

Master

Transfer curve
Shows the input-to-output curve of both stages together. The white dot rides on it with the live level, so you can see exactly where the music sits.
SC HPF
A high-pass filter on what both stages listen to (fully left = off, up to 400 Hz). Raise it so the bass doesn't make the whole mix pump; the bass itself isn't filtered.
MIX
Parallel compression: blends the dry signal back in. 100% is fully compressed; lower settings keep the transients of the dry signal under the compressed sound.
IN / OUT meters, TOTAL GR
Input and output level, and the combined gain reduction of both stages.

Using the key input (EXT KEY)

  1. On the track with Tandem, open ROUTE and set the track to 4 channels.
  2. On the track you want to duck from (e.g. the voice), add a send to Tandem's track and set its destination to channels 3/4.
  3. Switch EXT KEY on. KEY IN lights when the key signal arrives; lower Stage 1's THRESHOLD until the music dips by the amount you want.

Tips

  • Start with Stage 2's ratio at INF and its threshold just under your peaks: it becomes a safety limiter after Stage 1's gentle levelling.
  • If the low end makes the whole mix duck, raise SC HPF to 80-120 Hz.
  • Negative ratios sound best on sustained material (pads, rooms, reverb returns) and with MIX below 100%.
  • Use the transfer curve: if the white dot sits far up the flat part of the curve, you're compressing more than you probably need.

Modulation & space

Flanger

Three flangers in one: bucket-brigade pedal, wide-sweep rack and true through-zero.

Overview

Flanger mixes your signal with a copy of itself delayed by a few milliseconds, and sweeps that delay. The two combine into a comb of peaks and notches that moves up and down the spectrum - the swooshing, jet-plane sound.

Three characters are built in. PEDAL is the classic bucket-brigade stompbox: a darker loop with a little grit, made for strong regeneration. RACK is the clean, wide-sweep studio unit, going from metallic flange down to slow, chorus-like movement. THRU-0 recreates the original tape trick, where the swept copy passes right through the dry signal for a deep cancellation at the crossing point.

Besides the usual LFO, the sweep can be frozen so you can place the comb by hand (MATRIX), or driven by how hard you play (ENVELOPE).

Reach for it for

  • Jet-plane sweeps on guitars, synths and drum loops
  • Through-zero tape flanging on a whole mix or a bus
  • Hollow, nasal tones with negative regen
  • Static comb-filter colour with MATRIX
  • Touch-sensitive swooshes with ENVELOPE
  • Wide stereo motion with SPREAD

Controls

Modes (header)

PEDAL
The bucket-brigade pedal. The delay sweeps from about 0.3 to 6 ms, and the loop is darker with the delay chips' faint hiss. High REGEN gives the classic jet sound.
RACK
The wide-sweep studio rack: clean, sweeping from 0.15 to 14 ms. Short settings are metallic; long ones become a slow, chorus-like sweep.
THRU-0
Through-zero flanging, as done with two tape machines. The dry signal is delayed too, and the swept copy passes through it, giving the deep cancellation as it crosses. Best with MIX at 50%.

Flanger

SINE / TRI / SQR / RND
The LFO shape. Triangle is the classic even flanger sweep; sine lingers at the ends; square jumps between two settings; random wanders.
SWEEP
Click to choose what moves the sweep. LFO: the usual automatic sweep. MATRIX: the sweep stops and MANUAL places the comb by hand (the 'filter matrix' trick). ENVELOPE: your playing drives the sweep - louder notes push it further.
FREE / SYNC / FLOW
How the LFO rate is set: FREE in Hz, SYNC locked to the project tempo and timeline, FLOW tempo-synced but running continuously through loops. Not used in MATRIX or ENVELOPE.
RATE / SENS
The LFO speed (0.02 - 10 Hz, or a note value when synced). In ENVELOPE mode this knob becomes SENS: how far your playing pushes the sweep.
MANUAL
Where the sweep sits - the delay time at its centre. Low settings give high, metallic combs; high settings lower, more chorus-like tones.
WIDTH
How far the sweep travels either side of MANUAL. Zero gives a static comb (not used in MATRIX).
REGEN
Feedback, from -95% to +95%. Positive gives the ringing, metallic jet; negative gives a hollow, nasal tone. The level is compensated so strong settings don't jump out.
SPREAD
Offsets the right side's sweep from the left, from 0 to 180 degrees. 0 is a classic mono flange; 90 - 180 gives wide stereo motion.
Display
The live comb filter (frequency response) and the current delay time; MATRIX or ENVELOPE is shown when active.

Drift

A slow random wander, like a hand on the tape reel or an old clock.

RATE
How much the LFO rate wanders.
WIDTH
How much the sweep width wanders.
MANUAL
How much the centre of the sweep wanders.
DRIFT SPEED
How fast the drift moves (0.02 - 2 Hz).

Master

MIX
Dry / flanged balance. 50% gives the deepest notches; lower is subtler; 100% is the delayed signal only (a pitch-wobbling vibrato when sweeping).
OUTPUT
Output level, -24 to +12 dB.

Summing

Cassette 4

A cassette four-track as a summing mixer: four stereo stems recorded to tape and mixed down.

Overview

Cassette 4 is a summing mixer in the spirit of the 80s portable multitrack recorders. Each of four stereo stems goes to its own track of the tape - recorded, played back through the heads - and is then mixed down through the recorder's little mixer with its simple tone controls, pan and level.

Every track gets its own tape path: noise-reduction encoding, tape saturation, the wobble of the transport, the head's top-end loss and bump, dropouts and hiss, and the decoding on the way back. All four tracks share one transport, so they wow and flutter together, just like on a real machine.

Two models are on board. MODEL 15 is the compact standard-speed machine: soft top end, fat head bump, more wow and hiss. MODEL 44 is the double-speed machine with compander noise reduction: more top end and steadier, with the gentle breathing and pumping that compander noise reduction is known for.

Reach for it for

  • Lo-fi and bedroom-demo mixes
  • Giving a whole track that warm, wobbly cassette character
  • Gluing drums, keys and vocals with tape saturation
  • Pumping, breathing tape compression (MODEL 44)
  • Worn-out, nostalgic textures (AGE, HISS, WOW)

Setting up the routing in REAPER

  1. Create a bus track and put Cassette 4 on it. Open the track's ROUTE window and set Track channels to 8.
  2. On each stem track, add a send to the bus track and pick its destination channel pair in the send's audio routing: TRACK 1 on 1/2, TRACK 2 on 3/4, TRACK 3 on 5/6, TRACK 4 on 7/8.
  3. Turn off the master send on every stem track, so the stems are heard only through Cassette 4.
  4. TRACK 1 also receives whatever plays on the bus track itself (channels 1/2), so keep the bus track empty or use it as track 1.
  5. The mix leaves on channels 1/2, so leave the bus track's master send on. The green light under each track name lights when signal arrives.

Summing

M-Sum

An analogue broadcast-console summing bus with transformer iron and a fast VCA bus compressor.

Overview

M-Sum sums your stems the way an 80s British broadcast console's mix bus does. Up to eight stereo inputs - the bus track's own audio plus seven stems - are added together through the desk's bus physics, then pass through its output transformer and its fast VCA bus compressor.

The special part is the bus itself. The more energy you put on it, the further the virtual power rail sags (down to 65% at most), which acts as a soft, natural bus compression that responds to the whole mix rather than to any one stem. The transformer saturates only the lows below about 150 Hz, mostly with 3rd harmonic, and adds a small resonance at 35 Hz; the mids and highs pass through untouched.

Turned down, it is a clean summing mixer with solo and mute per stem. Pushed with DRIVE, IRON and SAG, it thickens, glues and bends like a desk running hot.

Reach for it for

  • Summing stems or groups through one console-style bus
  • Gluing a mix with a fast VCA bus compressor
  • Adding low-end weight and iron without dulling the top
  • Making a mix feel denser and more 'run through a desk' (SAG, DRIVE)
  • Quick stem balancing with solo and mute in one place

Setting up the routing in REAPER

  1. Create a mix bus track and put M-Sum on it. Open the track's ROUTE window and set Track channels to 16.
  2. On each stem track, add a send to the bus track. In the send's audio routing, pick a different destination channel pair for each stem: STEM 1 on 3/4, STEM 2 on 5/6, STEM 3 on 7/8, STEM 4 on 9/10, STEM 5 on 11/12, STEM 6 on 13/14, STEM 7 on 15/16.
  3. Turn off the master send on every stem track, so the stems are heard only through M-Sum (otherwise you hear them twice).
  4. Channels 1/2 are the MAIN input: whatever plays on the bus track itself (or is sent to it on 1/2). Use it for an eighth stem, or leave it empty.
  5. The summed mix leaves on channels 1/2, so leave the bus track's master send on. The green light above each input lights when signal arrives on it.

Controls

STEMS

Eight input strips: MAIN (channels 1-2) and stems 1 to 7 (channels 3-4 up to 15-16). The channel pair is printed under each number.

Signal light
The green light glows when audio arrives on that input and the input is being heard (not muted or left out by a solo).
Level knob
The input's level, from OFF (fully left, -60 dB) to +6 dB; 0 dB by default.
SOLO
Solo in place. Any number of inputs can be soloed at once; while anything is soloed, only the soloed inputs are heard.
MUTE
Silences the input. A muted input stays muted even when it is soloed.

CONSOLE BUS

DRIVE (yellow cap)
The level into the bus and the transformer, 0 to 24 dB. More DRIVE pushes the iron and the rail sag harder; it also raises the level, so bring OUTPUT down to compensate.
IRON (yellow cap)
How hard the output transformer's core works, 0 to 100 (15 by default). Only the lows below about 150 Hz are saturated, mostly with 3rd harmonic, for weight and density in the bottom end while the top stays clean.
SAG
How much the power rail sags under load, 0 to 0.30 (0.05 by default). The more energy on the bus, the lower the rail - a soft, programme-dependent squeeze. It reacts quickly (2 ms) and recovers in about 60 ms, so it feels like bus compression rather than distortion.
CROSSTALK
The left and right buses bleeding into each other, as on a real desk. Fully left is OFF; turn it up (to 0 dB) to narrow and glue the stereo image slightly.
STRESS
The orange light glows when the rail starts to sag.
RAIL
The bar and number show the rail voltage, from 100 (no sag) down to 65 (maximum sag). It turns orange when the rail drops below 90.

VCA BUS COMPRESSOR

A fast feed-forward compressor after the transformer. It reads the peaks of both sides and applies the same gain to both, so the image stays put.

THRESH (yellow cap)
The level where compression starts, -30 to 0 dB (-12 dB by default).
RATIO
1:1 to 20:1 (4:1 by default). 2:1 for gentle glue, 4:1 and up for obvious control.
ATTACK
0.1 to 20 ms (1 ms by default). Short times catch the transients; longer times let the punch through before it clamps down.
RELEASE
10 to 600 ms (150 ms by default). It is programme dependent: the longer the compressor keeps working (more than about 2 dB of reduction), the slower it releases, up to four times the set value, so sustained passages don't pump.
MAKEUP
Manual make-up gain, 0 to 18 dB. When AUTO is on, the knob reads AUTO and has no effect.
IN
Switches the compressor in or out (in by default).
AUTO
Automatic make-up: adds back half the reduction a full-scale peak would get, so the level stays roughly steady as you change THRESH and RATIO.
OUTPUT (yellow cap)
The final level of the bus, -24 to +12 dB. It works whether the compressor is in or out.
